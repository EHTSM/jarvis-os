### 5.3 Is 9950 seed, runtime-accumulation, test-generated, or mixed/unknown?

**Answer: Confirmed mixed/unknown, with only a partial lower bound establishable.**

- **Not seed/bootstrap** — ruled out definitively (§5.2, `DEFAULTS.economy.resourcePools = {}`).
- **Runtime-accumulation** — true by construction (every unit of the total arrived via a
  `contributeToPool`/`claimFromPool` call, there is no other write path).
- **Test-generated, partial lower bound only:** `civ-v9.test.cjs`'s own pool-test block
  (lines 254-266) nets **+50 capital per test run** (`contributeToPool(+100)` then
  `claimFromPool(-50)`, confirmed by direct read — this reproduces Mission 109's own finding
  unchanged). Given 199 known completed trades correspond to ~199 historic civ-v9 runs (per the
  758-key analysis in §3, `OrgA`/`OrgB`/`OrgC` triads number ~199-200 each), a **plausible partial
  lower bound is roughly 199 × 50 = 9,950** — **this arithmetic coincidence (9,950 vs the actual
  9950 figure) is striking but NOT proof**, because:
  1. There is no ledger (§5.4) to confirm the actual call count independent of the trade-count proxy.
  2. The HTTP routes (`civilizationOrg.js:68-69`) are an equally valid, unaudited write path with
     zero visibility into whether any non-test caller (a real operator action, a manual audit
     session, or another concurrent testing session) has also contributed or claimed against this
     same pool.
  3. Mission 102 already flagged this exact concern as unresolved (per Mission 109's own citation)
     and this mission's own independent check does not newly resolve it — it only sharpens the
     approximate arithmetic match, which was not previously stated this precisely.
- **Conclusion: mixed/unknown, with a plausible-but-unproven test-attributable component and no way
  to rule in or out a non-test contribution.**

entire total is accounted for by *some* combination of calls to these two functions — which was

### 5.6 Would changing it risk corrupting another subsystem?

**Direct trace of all readers of `getResourcePool`/`resourcePools`:** only `civilizationOrg.js:67`

## 7. Risks of Each Possible Action

- **Deleting the 758 civ-v9 balance keys (either subset):** Low technical risk (no other file
  references `economy.json`'s `balances` object by these specific keys — confirmed empty
  `trades: []`, and §4.2's cross-reference sweep found no `reputation.json`/other-file link to any of
  the 758 either, unlike the 1,615 set). Primary risk is precedent-setting: deleting the 398
  not-yet-fully-modeled keys means accepting provenance-only proof over exact-value proof, same
  standard Mission 105 already used for registry deletion — not a new risk category, but worth the
  founder explicitly re-confirming they're comfortable with that same standard applied to a second
  data type.
- **Deleting the 1,591 clean-dangling economy balance keys:** Low technical risk, directly mirrors
  Mission 105's already-certified registry approach on a structurally identical problem.
- **Deleting the 24 ambiguous economy balance keys without also handling `reputation.json`:** Real
  risk — leaves (or creates) a dangling reference in a second file, undermining the "no dangling
  reference" invariant Mission 105/108's own certification checks assert. Recommend these 24 be
  handled in the same mutation as their corresponding `reputation.json` badge records, not separately.
- **Deleting or fixing the 24 `reputation.json` badge records:** Same technical shape as the registry
  repairs already twice-certified (Missions 105, 108) — low risk if the same
  backup→hard-preservation-check→atomic-write→post-repair-validation discipline is reused. The 4
  BlueprintA-linked badges carry slightly elevated risk of being wrong, proportional to that pattern's
  already-disclosed INFERRED (not CONFIRMED) status.
- **Resetting `resourcePools.global` to 0 (or any other value):** No code-cascade risk (confirmed,
  §5.6) — but a genuine, irreversible **data-loss risk** to whatever legitimate portion (if any,
  unknown) the figure represents, with no way to ever recover or verify that portion after the fact.
  This is the highest-uncertainty action of all three decisions precisely because it's the one where
  "no ledger exists" means the founder cannot later audit whether the reset was correct.
- **Leaving all three categories exactly as-is indefinitely:** Zero technical risk, but perpetuates
  a known-inflated, partially-fictional total in both the economy balance ledger and the shared
  resource pool figure — a business-reporting-accuracy risk, not a code-safety risk.
